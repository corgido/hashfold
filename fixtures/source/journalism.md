# Town council postpones bridge vote after engineering report

A vote on the proposed replacement of the Kingsway Bridge was
deferred Tuesday night after the town council received an
engineering assessment that questioned the cost projections in the
original proposal.

The deferral was approved by a margin of seven to two, with the
two dissenting members arguing that further delay would expose
the town to additional liability if the existing structure failed
during the upcoming winter season.

"We are not refusing to act," said Council Chair Margaret Chen
after the vote. "We are refusing to act on numbers that haven't
been verified."

The engineering assessment, prepared by an outside firm at the
council's request, found that the cost of the proposed
replacement had been underestimated by between twelve and
eighteen percent in the original tender. The assessment cited
recent increases in the cost of structural steel and noted that
the project's timeline did not include a contingency for winter
weather delays.

The original tender, submitted in March by Harrow & Sons
Construction, had proposed a thirty-month build at a fixed cost
of four point two million dollars. The revised figure suggested
by the assessment puts the likely cost at between four point
seven and four point nine million.

A representative for Harrow & Sons declined to comment on the
revised figures Tuesday night but said the firm would provide a
formal response within ten business days.

The Kingsway Bridge, which carries the town's main north-south
route across the Allen River, was first identified as a
candidate for replacement in 2019, when an inspection report
classified it as "fair to poor" and noted that several of the
load-bearing elements had been operating beyond their design
life. A subsequent emergency repair in 2022 extended the
structure's serviceable life by an estimated five to seven
years, but did not alter the underlying need for replacement.

Council member Robert Fletcher, one of the two dissenting voters,
said after the meeting that the deferral risked making the bridge
itself a political issue rather than an engineering one.

"Every winter we leave it, we are taking a chance that we don't
need to take," Fletcher said. "I understand the chair's
concerns about the cost, but the cost of not building is the
cost of the bridge plus the cost of whatever happens if we wait
too long."

Chen, asked about Fletcher's comments, said she did not disagree
with his framing of the risk but disagreed with his conclusion.

"I want this bridge built," Chen said. "I want it built on a
budget we can actually meet, by a contractor whose figures we
can actually verify. We are not going to make a generation's
worth of payments on numbers that nobody on this council
understands."

The council is scheduled to revisit the proposal at its next
meeting, on November 14th. The engineering firm has been asked
to attend that meeting and to present its findings in person.
Harrow & Sons has been invited to respond.

Residents of the affected neighbourhoods, who packed the council
chamber for Tuesday's vote, were divided in their reactions. A
group from the Riverside Association, which represents the
neighbourhoods immediately north of the bridge, distributed a
statement after the vote calling the deferral "responsible but
overdue." A separate group of business owners from the south
side of the river expressed frustration that the project had
been delayed yet again.

"We have been hearing about this bridge for five years," said
Linda Park, owner of a hardware store on the south side. "Every
year there is a reason to wait. Every year the bridge gets
worse. At some point we have to actually build it."

The town's public works director, James Okafor, was not present
at Tuesday's meeting. A statement issued by his office said the
department was reviewing the engineering assessment and would
provide its own analysis at the November 14th meeting.

In the meantime, weight restrictions on the existing bridge
remain in effect, and trucks over fifteen tonnes are required
to use the alternate route via Henderson Road. The detour adds
approximately twelve minutes to most commercial deliveries.
